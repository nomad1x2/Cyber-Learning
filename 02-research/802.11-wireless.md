# IEEE 802.11

Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE)
- Establishes industry standards in electrical engineering, electronics, and related fields
- 802 working group covers LAN / PAN / MAN tech
- 802.11 specifically defines WLAN

## 802 series standards

Standards for:
- Local Area Networks (LANs)
- Personal Area Networks (PANs)
- Metropolitan Area Networks (MANs)

Ref:
- https://www.signalboosters.com/blog/what-are-the-wifi-ieee-80211-standards/

## 802.11 standards

Specifies the set of MAC and physical layer protocols for implementing wireless local area networks (WLANs)

|Standard|Name|Bands|Channel Width|Max rates|Notes|
|----|----|----|----|----|----|
|802.11|-|2.4 GHz|20 MHz|1–2 Mbps|first WLAN standard in 1999|
|802.11a|Wifi 2|5 GHz|20 MHz|54 Mbps|implemented Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) to encode data on multiple frequencies|
|802.11b|Wifi 1|2.4 GHz|20 MHz|11 Mbps|implemented Direct-Sequence Spread Spectrum (DSSS) to reduce signal interference - not compatible with 802.11a|
|802.11g|Wifi 3|2.4 GHz|20 MHz|54 Mbps|took best qualities from 802.11a/b - only compatible with 802.11b|
|802.11n|Wireless-N or Wifi 4|2.4/5 GHz|20/40MHz|up to 600 Mbps|MIMO,64-QAM|
|802.11ac|Gigabit Wifi or Wifi 5|5 GHz|20/40/80/160 MHz|up to ~6.9 Gbps|implemented Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO) to allow multiple receive - compatible with 802.11a/b/g|
|802.11ad|WiGig|60 GHz|~2.16 GHzchannels|up to ~7 Gbps|designed to provide Multiple Gigabit Wireless System (MGWS) - devices need to be within a short range from the AP (short range mmWave)|
|802.11ah|Wifi HaLow|sub 1 GHz|narrow|up to ~347 Mbps|lower energy consumption with increased range|
|802.11ax|Wifi 6/6E|2.4/5/6 GHz|20–160MHz|up to ~9.6 Gbps|implements OFDMA, MU-MIMO, 1024-QAM, and more - compatible with 802.11a/b/g/n/ac|

## Channel Width

Wider channel = higher throughput, but more interference risk

# CSMA/CA

Carrier Sense Multpiple Access / Collision Avoidance
- Used to manage how data packets are transmitted and to avoid collisions between them
- Allows devices to "sense" the channel for traffic before transmitting

## Notes

- In general, WLANs are more susceptible to interference from other devices
- Distance impacts bandwidth and speeds
